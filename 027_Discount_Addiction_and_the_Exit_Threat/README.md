# Case #27: Discount Addiction and the Exit Threat

![Cover Image](images/53.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case illustrates **Price Erosion** and the failure of "Value-Based Pricing." By yielding to constant discount demands, the company has compromised its **Net Margin per Customer**. This creates a dangerous precedent where the client perceives the service as a commodity. Technically, the "Contribution Margin" is no longer sufficient to justify the risk of churn. The company is experiencing "Negative Price Variance," which is the most difficult financial trend to reverse once established.

![Financial Proof](images/54.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. **Discount Caps:** Establish a firm policy that discounts cannot exceed a certain percentage without a corresponding reduction in service scope.
2. **Value Quantification:** Present the client with a "Business Value Report" that highlights the ROI they receive, moving the conversation away from unit price.
3. **Managed Churn:** Be prepared to let a low-margin, high-threat client leave to free up capacity for clients who pay for value.
