# Case #11: High Profits and Negative Cash Flow

![Cover Image](images/21.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case explores the fundamental difference between **Accrual Accounting** and **Cash Basis Accounting**. A company recognizes revenue when earned and expenses when incurred, leading to "Net Income" on the P&L. However, if that revenue is tied up in Accounts Receivable (uncollected) or if cash is spent on excess Inventory, the Net Income does not translate into spendable cash.

From a **CMA (Certified Management Accountant)** perspective, this is a crisis of **Working Capital Management**. A business can be "Net Income Positive" but "Operating Cash Flow Negative," leading to technical insolvency. The "Quality of Earnings" is low because the profit is not backed by cash, which is often a precursor to a liquidity crisis or the need for expensive external financing.

![Financial Proof](images/22.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. **Indirect Cash Flow Preparation:** Frequently reconcile Net Income to Operating Cash Flow to identify exactly where the cash is being trapped.
2. **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) Optimization:** Reduce the time it takes to convert inventory and receivables back into cash.
3. **Working Capital KPIs:** Move away from pure P&L management and include Operating Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow in executive performance metrics.
4. **Stricter Credit & Inventory Policies:** Tighten credit terms for slow-paying customers and implement Just-in-Time (JIT) inventory to prevent cash from rotting in the warehouse.
