# Case #6: The Squeezed Margin of Strategic Partnerships

![Cover Image](images/11.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case illustrates "Concentration Risk" and the erosion of bargaining power. When a company relies on a single strategic partner for the majority of its growth, it loses its ability to maintain price integrity. The partner, aware of their dominant position, can force a price squeeze that eliminates the Net Margin.

From a CMA (Certified Management Accountant) perspective, this is a failure to manage the "Bargaining Power of Buyers" within Porter's Five Forces framework. While the partnership increases volume, it fails the "Contribution Margin" test if the servicing costs (support, customization, and API maintenance) rise as the unit price falls. Revenue is growing, but the business is becoming structurally weaker.

![Financial Proof](images/12.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. Revenue Diversification: Set a strategic cap where no single partner or client accounts for more than 20% of total revenue.
2. Unit Economics Audit: Perform a deep dive into the specific servicing costs associated with partnership deals to ensure positive Net Margin.
3. Performance-Based Tiers: Structure future contracts with price tiers that protect the margin as volume increases.
4. Exit Strategy: Maintain a "stand-alone" sales team to ensure the company can survive if the strategic partnership is terminated.
