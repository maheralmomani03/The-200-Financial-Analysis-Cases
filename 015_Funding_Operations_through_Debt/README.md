# Case #15: Funding Operations through Debt

![Cover Image](images/29.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case highlights the "Debt Dependency" trap where a company appears profitable on an accrual basis but is functionally insolvent on a cash basis. The company is experiencing a "Cash Burn" in its core operations, which it hides by constantly injecting liquidity from external financing. This creates a dangerous spike in financial leverage and interest expense.

From a **CMA (Certified Management Accountant)** perspective, the critical ratio to monitor here is the **Operating Cash Flow Ratio**:
$$Operating\ Cash\ Flow\ Ratio = \frac{Operating\ Cash\ Flow}{Current\ Liabilities}$$
A ratio below 1.0 indicates that the company is not generating enough cash to pay off its short-term debt. When a firm uses debt to fund operating losses, it enters a "Death Spiral" where increasing interest payments further erode net income, requiring even more debt to survive.

![Financial Proof](images/30.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. **Cash Flow Sustainability Audit:** Identify the "Burn Rate" of operations and set a hard deadline for the business to reach "Cash Flow Break-Even."
2. **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Monitoring:** Ensure the DSCR remains well above 1.25 to guarantee that the business can cover its debt obligations from its own earnings.
3. **Operational Turnaround:** Focus on reducing variable costs and improving collection cycles (DSO) to generate internal cash rather than seeking external loans.
4. **Capital Restructuring:** If debt levels are unsustainable, consider equity financing or asset liquidation to pay down high-interest short-term lines of credit.
