# Case #7: Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) vs. Profit

![Cover Image](images/13.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case highlights a common failure in "Growth Accounting." While the marketing department focuses on "Customer Count," the finance department must focus on the **LTV/CAC Ratio** (Lifetime Value to Customer Acquisition Cost). If the cost to acquire a customer exceeds the immediate contribution margin (and there is no significant recurring revenue), the business is experiencing "Negative Unit Economics."

From a **CMA (Certified Management Accountant)** perspective, this is a breakdown in **Cost-Volume-Profit (CVP)** analysis. Marketing spend should be treated as a variable cost tied to sales volume in this context. If the marginal cost of acquisition is greater than the marginal profit per unit, the company's "Margin of Safety" is non-existent. Scaling in this environment only accelerates the burn rate and depletes equity.

![Financial Proof](images/14.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. **CAC Payback Period Analysis:** Calculate exactly how many months/transactions it takes to recover the acquisition cost. If it exceeds 12 months, the marketing strategy must be overhauled.
2. **Channel Optimization:** Pivot spending from high-CAC paid channels to lower-cost organic or referral strategies to balance the blended CAC.
3. **Price Adjustments:** If CAC cannot be lowered, increase the product price or bundle services to raise the "Average Order Value" (AOV) and cover the acquisition cost.
4. **LTV Improvement:** Focus on retention and cross-selling to existing customers to maximize their Lifetime Value, making the initial high CAC more justifiable over time.
