# Case #13: Accounts Receivable (AR) Expansion

![Cover Image](images/25.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case illustrates the "AR Ballooning" effect, where a company's revenue growth is disconnected from its collection efficiency. While the P&L shows a 30% increase in revenue, the Balance Sheet reveals that the assets are becoming increasingly "illiquid." The capital is trapped in the hands of customers rather than being available for reinvestment.

From a CMA (Certified Management Accountant) perspective, the Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) is the critical early-warning metric. A DSO jump from 55 to 126 days indicates a severe breakdown in the credit department or a desperate attempt to gain market share by offering unsustainable terms. This "Paper Profit" creates a phantom tax liability and a real cash flow drain, as the company must still pay its vendors and employees in cash, not in receivables.

![Financial Proof](images/26.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. DSO-Linked Incentives: Restructure sales bonuses to be contingent on successful cash collection within 60 days.
2. Credit Limit Lockdown: Immediately freeze additional credit for any client whose balance exceeds 90 days past due.
3. Automated Collection Workflows: Implement an automated system for payment reminders and escalation for high-value accounts receivable.
4. AR Factoring Review: If the cash gap becomes critical, evaluate the cost of factoring these receivables to unlock the liquidity needed for operations.
