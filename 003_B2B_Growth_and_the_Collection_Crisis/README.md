# Case #3: B2B Growth and the Collection Crisis

![Cover Image](images/5.png)

### 📝 Technical Analysis
This case illustrates the "Overtrading" phenomenon, where a business grows faster than its ability to fund its working capital. The disconnect between **Accrual Accounting** (Revenue Recognition) and **Cash Flow realization** creates a "Cash Gap." As the company signs more B2B contracts with long payment terms, the **Accounts Receivable (AR)** balance balloons, absorbing all available liquidity.

From a **CMA (Certified Management Accountant)** perspective, the critical metric here is the **Days Sales Outstanding (DSO)** and the **Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)**. A rising DSO during a growth phase is a red flag indicating that the company is essentially providing interest-free loans to its customers at the expense of its own survival. Without a strong collection strategy, high-revenue growth leads directly to technical insolvency.

![Financial Proof](images/6.png)

### 💡 The Correct Action
1. **DSO Monitoring:** Set strict thresholds for Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) and trigger immediate alerts when they are exceeded.
2. **Incentivize Collections:** Re-align the sales commission structure to be paid only upon "Cash Collection" rather than "Contract Signing."
3. **Credit Risk Management:** Implement more rigorous credit checks for new B2B clients and offer small discounts (e.g., 2/10 net 30) to encourage early payments.
4. **AR Factoring:** If cash flow becomes critical, consider invoice factoring as a short-term tool to unlock trapped liquidity, despite the cost.
